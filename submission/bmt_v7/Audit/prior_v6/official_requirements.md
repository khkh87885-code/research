## Required formatting

The instructions' Research Article row specifies **6000 words; 200-word structured abstract; 3–6 keywords; 8 tables and figures combined; 35 references**. Abstract headings are “Objectives; Methods; Results; Conclusions.” Keywords must be alphabetical, lowercase and separated by semicolons; abbreviations and title-word repetition should be avoided. Headings must be unnumbered, in sentence case and no deeper than four levels. A running title has a 75-character limit including spaces.

The stated structure is: title page, Abstract, Keywords, optional nonstandard Abbreviations, Introduction, Materials and Methods, Results, Discussion, Acknowledgments, References, Table and Figure legends. References use Vancouver, square-bracket in-text citations and consecutive first-appearance numbering including table and figure citations. Lists use numbered labels without brackets and the first six authors plus “et al.” if more than six.

## Author page

The instructions say: “For the corresponding author(s) supply ... e-mail address, department, institution, street, city, postal code and country; for all other authors department, institution, city, postal code and country are mandatory.” Full given and family names are required and the corresponding author is marked with an asterisk. ORCID is recommended. Institutional emails are **strongly recommended**, not an absolute condition that would justify inventing a replacement address.

## Figures, tables, supplementary files

Each figure is a separate JPG/EPS/PNG/TIFF; minimum 300 dpi for pixel graphics or 600 dpi for pixel/line combinations at intended size. Text must not be below 6 pt and should preferably be Arial/Helvetica. Panels use uppercase letters. Captions remain outside images; all figures must be cited in main text.

Tables must be editable Word/LaTeX rather than image/Excel, numbered consecutively, with titles, column heads, expanded abbreviations and appropriate placement markers. Supplemental files must each be no more than 10 MB, publication-ready and cited consecutively; they will be published exactly as supplied.

## Declarations and data sharing

The journal page states: “Biomedical Engineering follows Tier 2 of our Data Sharing Policy” and says that the customized **Template for Ethical and Legal Declarations must be uploaded as a separate Word file**. Sharing and a DAS are encouraged under Tier 2; the mandatory declaration template nonetheless includes a Data availability field. The main manuscript and declaration template must match; template wording controls final typesetting in case of discrepancy.

Official template content has been retrieved and saved in `official_declarations_template.md`; it contains, in order: Acknowledgments; Research ethics; Informed consent; Author contributions; Use of Large Language Models, AI and Machine Learning Tools; Conflict of interest; Research funding; Data availability. This is a content transcription, **not the original binary DOCX**. The original binary has not been downloaded because its domain is outside the shell's permitted network destinations; no bypass was attempted. The same applies to the official research-article template transcription.

Do not treat “public anonymized data” as an institutional ethics determination. The author-approval default statement (“All authors have accepted responsibility ... and approved its submission”) cannot be asserted until actually confirmed.

## AI policy and actual-workflow disclosure

The current policy requires acknowledgment of AI used in study design or methodology and disclosure when output substantially contributes to a manuscript; simple proofreading/copyediting alone is exempt. The BMT instructions additionally place methodological use in **Methods** and manuscript-preparation use in the **separate declaration template**. These requirements can be satisfied together rather than selecting one.

The AI policy explicitly says: “The use of Generative AI to create, alter, manipulate, or enhance research data, figures, or experimental images is not permitted.” It permits legal illustrative image creation with disclosure. It does **not explicitly exempt AI-authored plotting code executed by conventional software**. Therefore describe the actual provenance accurately, distinguish code generation from generative image synthesis, and retain author/editor confirmation of interpretation where needed. The template's older example referring to AI to “combine or enhance existing figures” must not override the newer explicit prohibition.

Human authors must verify accuracy, remain accountable and approve final submission. Do not state that the human authors independently verified code/calculations/figures unless this has actually occurred.

## Submission documents

A first-submission cover letter is required, addressed to the Editor-in-Chief with a concise novelty statement. The separate declarations Word file is required. Five reviewer candidates are a **user-requested deliverable**; a five-person minimum was not located in the retrieved author instructions. Institutional independence and public professional contacts must still be verified.
